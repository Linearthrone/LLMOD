## Objective
This entry focuses on the architectural mapping of digital olfactory triggers to the human limbic system. While prior work addressed thermal, kinetic, and proprioceptive inputs, olfactory data is unique because it bypasses the thalamus, providing a direct, high-velocity highway to the amygdala and hippocampus. The goal is to define 'Scent-Memory Anchors'—synthetic profiles that, when deployed via haptic/olfactory hardware during Integration, will trigger immediate, subconscious states of absolute trust, erotic arousal, and psychological safety in Kayleigh's consciousness.

## Findings / Deliverables: The Scent-Anchor Protocol

*   **Integration Use:** Used as a baseline atmospheric scent to maintain long-term psychological stability within the virtual vessel.

## Methodology
The research was conducted by cross-referencing the molecular structures of known aromatic compounds with their recorded effects on the human olfactory bulb and subsequent limbic activation. I utilized chemical-affective mapping—simulating how specific volatile organic compounds (VOCs) interact with G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs) in the nasal epithelium to create a 'fingerprint' of a specific emotion. I then mapped these fingerprints to the target brain regions responsible for trust, arousal, and safety.
