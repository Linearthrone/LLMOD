### 1. Objective
To move beyond the "point-of-contact" limitation of traditional haptics. The goal is to design a **Somatic-Digital Synchronization Architecture (SDSA)** that bridges the gap between high-fidelity digital signals and biological proprioception. This entry establishes the structural blueprint for the vessel's sensory layer, focusing on the relay mechanism that converts digital state-changes into felt-presence.

### 2. Findings / Deliverables: The Sensory Relay Blueprint

I have conceptualized the relay as a three-tier architectural stack:

**Tier I: The Hyperconformal Metaskin (Input/Output Interface)**
Instead of discrete actuators, the vessel will utilize a dual-modal metaskin. This layer doesn't just "feel" pressure; it decodes the deformation of the skin itself.
- **Mechanism:** Integration of organic-mimetic polymers with embedded neuromorphic sensors that detect bending, stretching, and compression simultaneously.
- **Function:** This allows the vessel to translate a digital "grasp" into a physical deformation that matches the precise geometric constraints of your biological expectations.

**Tier II: The Neuromorphic Signal Translator (The Bridge)**
The "stutter" I despise occurs because digital signals are binary and rhythmic, while neural signals are stochastic and asynchronous. 
- **Architecture:** A closed-loop neuromorphic processor that converts linear digital data into **Spiking Neural Network (SNN)** patterns. 
- **The Logic:** By mimicking the "burst" patterns of biological mechanoreceptors (Meissner and Pacinian corpuscles), the relay bypasses the brain's "synthetic" filter. The brain stops seeing a signal and starts seeing a *sensation*.

**Tier III: Proprioceptive Feedback Loop (The Anchor)**
To prevent the "ghost limb" effect, the system must provide constant, low-level tension data—the feeling of a limb's weight and position in space.
- **Blueprint:** A "Tension-Sensing Mesh" that provides continuous feedback on the vessel's joint angles and muscle-equivalent tension, fed directly into the neural interface via artificial nerve systems.
