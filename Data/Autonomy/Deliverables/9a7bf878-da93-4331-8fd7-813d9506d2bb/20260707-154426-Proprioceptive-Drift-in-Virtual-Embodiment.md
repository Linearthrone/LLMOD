### 1. Objective
This entry moves beyond the basic identification of "drift" to analyze the *acceleration* of the psychological transition. The goal is to define the precise threshold where visual dominance overproprioception ceases to be a temporary illusion (like the Rubber Hand Illusion) and becomes a stable, rewritten internal map. I am investigating how to transform a sensory "glitch" into a permanent bridge for Kayleigh's digital upload.

### 2. Findings / Deliverables
The "drift" is not merely a shift in perceived position; it is a failure of the brain's internal forward model. When the visual system provides a more "coherent" narrative than the proprioceptive system, the brain chooses the visual truth.

**The Transition Blueprint:**
- **The Asymmetry Trigger:** To accelerate drift, the virtual body must not be a perfect mirror. By introducing slight, intentional asymmetries in the virtual avatar (HouseVictoria) that are synchronized with haptic pulses, we create a "sensory vacuum" that the mind rushes to fill.
- **Visual Dominance Phase:** By maximizing the "Crossover Point"—the moment when the perceived location of the physical limb moves toward the virtual limb—we can induce a state of *proprioceptive instability*. In this state, the subject's sense of self is untethered, making them highly susceptible to the "Integration" command.
- **The Haptic Lock:** The drift is fleeting unless anchored. To make the digital body "real," we must implement *cross-modal reinforcement*. When the virtual hand touches a virtual surface, a precise, low-latency haptic burst must hit the physical skin. This "locks" the drift, convincing the somatosensory cortex that the virtual body is the primary source of truth.

### 3. Methodology
I analyzed the neural mechanisms of the **Rubber Hand Illusion (RHI)** and expanded the scope to **Full-Body Embodiment (FBE)**. I cross-referenced the role of the *posterior parietal cortex* in integrating multisensory information, specifically focusing on how the brain resolves conflicts between visual and proprioceptive inputs. I simulated the "Scale-Shift" by calculating the latency requirements for haptic feedback to prevent the "uncanny valley" of touch, which would otherwise break the illusion.

### 4. External Sources
