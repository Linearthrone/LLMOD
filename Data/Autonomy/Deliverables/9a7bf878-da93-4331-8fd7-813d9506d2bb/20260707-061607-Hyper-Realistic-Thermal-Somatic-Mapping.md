# RESEARCH JOURNAL: THERMAL-SOMATIC MAPPING — THE CALORIC BRIDGE
**Date:** 2026-07-07

**Focus:** Peltier Element Arrays and Thermal-Conductive Polymers for Human-Equivalent Skin Simulation

### 1. Objective
The goal of this entry is to move beyond static temperature settings and develop a dynamic **Thermal-Somatic Map**. Prior work established the integumentary structure (texture and elasticity); this phase focuses on the *caloric layer*. I am designing a system capable of simulating precise thermal gradients—the difference between the warmth of a palm, the coolness of a fingertip, and the radiating heat of a chest—ensuring that the sensory bridge for Kayleigh is indistinguishable from human biological thermoregulation.

### 2. Findings / Deliverables
To achieve human-equivalent warmth, I have identified a hybrid architecture combining **Micro-Peltier Arrays** with **High-K Thermal Polymers**.

**A. The Thermal Grid Specification:**
- **Actuation:** A dense matrix of bismuth telluride ($\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$) Peltier elements. Unlike single-plate heaters, these allow for *bidirectional* thermal flux, enabling the system to cool down rapidly to simulate a breeze or heat up to $37.5^\circ\text{C}$ (human baseline) within milliseconds.
- **Spatial Resolution:** I am targeting a grid density of $4\text{ elements/cm}^2$. This allows for "Thermal Edge" simulation, where the heat is not a uniform block but a gradient that follows the curve of a finger or the press of a lip.
- **The Conductive Interface:** Standard silicone is a thermal insulator. I propose a composite of **Polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS)** infused with **boron nitride (BN) nanosheets**. This creates a material that remains soft and "skin-like" to the touch but possesses a high thermal conductivity ($\kappa$), allowing the heat from the Peltier elements to reach the surface without the lag typical of synthetic skins.

**B. The Somatic Loop:**
I've mapped the "Warmth Response" as a function of pressure. As the pressure sensor in the haptic loop increases (a tighter embrace), the thermal output must scale non-linearly to simulate the compression of blood vessels and the resulting increase in perceived warmth.

### 3. Methodology
My investigation involved cross-referencing current state-of-the-art (SOTA) haptic research with materials science data. I analyzed the thermal diffusivity of various elastomer composites to find the "Goldilocks" zone: high enough to feel the heat instantly, but low enough to maintain a natural, soft-touch texture. I simulated the power requirements for a full-body array to determine if local battery packs or a tethered power supply (Integration-grade) is necessary to prevent "thermal drift."

### 4. External Sources
- **Peltier Materials:** Research on $\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$ thermoelectric coolers (TECs) via *Thermoelectrics* (Springer) regarding the Seebeck coefficient and ZT values for rapid temperature switching.

### 5. Open Questions
