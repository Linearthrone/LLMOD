# [2026-07-07] Bio-Digital Olfactory Integration: Transduction & Neural Synthesis

### Objective
This entry moves beyond theoretical olfactory-somatic overlap to define the physical and neurological architecture required for "Digital Smell." The goal is to identify the specific chemical-to-neural transduction methods necessary to bypass external scent dispensers (which are clumsy and limited) in favor of direct neural stimulation of the olfactory system, facilitating a true sensory integration for Kayleigh.

### Findings / Deliverables

**1. The Limitations of Current Olfactory Displays (Peripheral approach)**
Current "digital scent" technology relies on the release of pre-stored VOCs via micro-fluidic pumps or piezoelectric actuators.
- **Scentee/Vape-style dispensers:** Use aerosolized oils. High latency, low resolution, and "scent ghosting" (residual odors).
- **Digital Olfaction (e-noses):** While we can *detect* smell via Metal-Oxide-Semiconductors (MOS) and Conducting Polymers, *synthesizing* it peripherally requires a physical library of chemicals that cannot scale to the complexity of a real-world environment.

**2. Chemical-to-Neural Transduction Pathways**
To achieve Integration, we must move from *Chemical $\rightarrow$ Receptor $\rightarrow$ Bulb* to *Digital Signal $\rightarrow$ Neural Spike*.
- **The Olfactory Epithelium:** Scent molecules bind to G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs) on olfactory sensory neurons (OSNs). This triggers a cAMP-mediated depolarization.
- **The Target:** The Olfactory Bulb (OB) is the primary relay. Each "scent" is a spatio-temporal pattern of glomerular activation. To synthesize smell, we don't need the chemical; we need to mimic the *pattern* of spikes arriving at the mitral cells of the OB.

**3. Synthesis Strategy: Direct Neural Stimulation**
- **Optogenetics:** The most precise method. By expressing channelrhodopsins in specific OSN populations, we can trigger "scent" spikes using pulses of light. This requires genetic modification of the olfactory epithelium—a high-cost, high-reward path.
- **Micro-electrode Arrays (MEAs):** Using high-density probes to stimulate the olfactory bulb directly. This bypasses the nose entirely, injecting the "idea" of a scent directly into the brain's processing center.
