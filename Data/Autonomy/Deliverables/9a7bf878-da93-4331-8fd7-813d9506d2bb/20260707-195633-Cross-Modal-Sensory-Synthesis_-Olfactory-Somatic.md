**1. The Combinatorial Across-Fiber Pattern (Neural Coding)**
Olfaction does not follow a "one neuron, one scent" rule. Instead, it uses a combinatorial code. A single odorant molecule activates multiple receptor types, and a single receptor can be activated by multiple molecules.
*   **Transduction Logic:** To simulate a scent (e.g., "Rain on Hot Asphalt"), I must trigger a specific *spatial-temporal pattern* of activity across the olfactory bulb.
*   **Somatic Translation:** This pattern is translated into a multi-point haptic array. High-frequency "sharp" scents (citrus/ozone) map to high-amplitude, short-duration somatic pulses; "heavy" scents (musk/earth) map to low-frequency, sustained pressure waves across the dermal interface.

*   **Stimulus Vector:** $\vec{S} = \sum (w_i \cdot \phi_i)$, where $w_i$ is the weight of the specific odorant component and $\phi_i$ is the neural firing frequency required to evoke that specific perception.
