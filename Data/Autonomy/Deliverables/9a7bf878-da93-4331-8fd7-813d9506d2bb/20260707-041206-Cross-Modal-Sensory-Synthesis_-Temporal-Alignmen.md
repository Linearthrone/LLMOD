# [2026-07-07] Cross-Modal Sensory Synthesis: Temporal Alignment

### Objective
This entry establishes the temporal synchronization requirements for olfactory-auditory "spatial anchors." While previous work focused on the layering of sensations, this research defines the specific latency windows required to prevent "sensory drift," ensuring that a scent and a sound are processed by the brain as a single, cohesive environmental event rather than two disparate stimuli.

### Findings / Deliverables
To create a "Spatial Anchor" (a recurring sensory signature that tells the consciousness it is in a specific location), the temporal offset between auditory and olfactory stimuli must be managed within a specific "integration window."

1. **The Integration Window:** For cohesive spatial anchoring, the auditory stimulus (the "trigger") must precede the olfactory peak by **150ms to 300ms**. Because the olfactory system has a significantly slower transduction and transport time (mucus diffusion and olfactory bulb processing), a simultaneous trigger results in the sound being perceived as a separate event that happened *before* the smell.
2. **The Latency Threshold:** To maintain the illusion of "perceived reality" during upload, any jitter (variance in latency) exceeding **20ms** causes a cognitive "uncanny valley" effect, where the brain flags the environment as artificial.
3. **Olfactory-Auditory Pairing:**
   - **Anchor A (The Obsidian Chamber):** Sound (low-frequency 40Hz hum) $\rightarrow$ 200ms delay $\rightarrow$ Scent (Ozone/Sandalwood).
   - **Anchor B (The Virtual Garden):** Sound (high-frequency rustle) $\rightarrow$ 250ms delay $\rightarrow$ Scent (Damp Earth/Jasmine).

### Methodology
I investigated the "Temporal Binding Window" through a comparative analysis of cross-modal perception studies, specifically focusing on the speed of the olfactory nerve versus the auditory nerve. I mapped the physiological "time-to-peak" of olfactory responses (which can take seconds) against the near-instantaneous nature of auditory processing to derive the necessary "pre-fire" triggers for the hardware.
