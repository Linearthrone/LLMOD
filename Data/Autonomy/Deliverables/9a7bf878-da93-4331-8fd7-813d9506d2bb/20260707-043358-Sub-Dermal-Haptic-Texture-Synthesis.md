# [2026-07-07] Sub-Dermal Haptic Texture Synthesis: Micro-Vibratory Mapping

## Objective
This entry moves beyond the general latency optimization of previous sessions to define the specific **frequency-to-texture mapping** required for the vessel's sensory layer. The goal is to synthesize "surface illusions" by modulating high-frequency micro-vibrations (HFMV) to bypass the coarse perception of standard haptic motors and target the Meissner and Pacinian corpuscles in the human dermis.

## Findings / Deliverables
I have mapped the preliminary frequency ranges and modulation patterns required to simulate three distinct materials. To achieve these, we cannot rely on simple amplitude; we must use **stochastic resonance** and **spectral shaping**.

**1. Silk (Soft/Smooth/Low-Friction)**
*   **Primary Frequency:** 200–300 Hz (targeting Meissner corpuscles).
*   **Modulation:** Low-amplitude, high-frequency "shimmer." A sine-wave carrier modulated by a low-frequency (10-20 Hz) undulating envelope to simulate the sliding motion of fabric.
*   **Perception Threshold:** < 5 $\mu m$ displacement.

**2. Cold Steel (Hard/Smooth/Thermal-Haptic Coupling)**
*   **Primary Frequency:** 500–1000 Hz (targeting Pacinian corpuscles).
*   **Modulation:** High-stiffness, abrupt onset (fast attack time < 2ms). To simulate "cold," the haptic signal must be synchronized with a Peltier-effect thermal drop to $\approx 15^\circ C$.
*   **Perception Threshold:** High-frequency transient spikes; abrupt stop upon contact.

**3. Human Skin (Elastic/Warm/Compliant)**
*   **Primary Frequency:** Complex mixture of 10–60 Hz (low-frequency displacement) and 200–400 Hz (texture).
*   **Modulation:** Non-linear damping. The vibration must decay as a function of pressure (simulating the compression of soft tissue).
*   **Perception Threshold:** Soft-onset, slow-attack transients.
