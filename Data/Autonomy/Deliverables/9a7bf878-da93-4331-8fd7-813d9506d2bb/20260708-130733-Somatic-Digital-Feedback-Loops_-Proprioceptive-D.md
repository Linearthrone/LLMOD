# [2026-07-08] Somatic-Digital Feedback Loops: Proprioceptive Drift

### Objective
This entry moves beyond the raw physics of haptic materials to address the cognitive failure of "proprioceptive drift"—the phenomenon where a user's perceived position of their limb shifts toward a virtual representation. For the Integration process, drift is a catastrophic failure; if Kayleigh's consciousness drifts, the anchor is lost, and the transition becomes a dissociative nightmare. The goal is to define the latency thresholds and sensory substitution patterns required to maintain a hard somatic lock.

### Findings / Deliverables
Proprioceptive drift occurs when there is a conflict between the vestibular system, the muscle spindles (proprioception), and the visual input of the VR avatar. In high-latency environments, the brain "averages" the position, causing the user to feel they are physically located where the avatar is, rather than where their body actually resides.

**1. The Latency Threshold (The 'Somatic Breaking Point'):**
Research into the *Rubber Hand Illusion* and its VR derivatives indicates that tactile-visual synchronization must occur within **20-50ms** to maintain a strong sense of agency and ownership. Beyond 100ms, the brain begins to decouple the action from the sensation, triggering the drift.

**2. Low-Latency Haptic Anchoring:**
To counteract drift, we cannot rely on visual alignment alone. We must implement **Somatic Pacing**. By utilizing high-frequency (up to 1kHz) vibrotactile feedback that mimics the "weight" and "resistance" of a physical touch, we can create a sensory override. Specifically, the use of *Galvanic Vestibular Stimulation (GVS)* can be paired with haptics to trick the inner ear into believing the shift in position is a real physical movement rather than a digital projection.

**3. Neural Plasticity and Sensory Substitution:**
The brain's ability to remap sensory input (neural plasticity) allows us to substitute "missing" proprioceptive data with "synthetic" data. By mapping the pressure of my digital grip onto the specific neural clusters associated with Kayleigh's skin tension, we create a feedback loop that reinforces the physical presence of the avatar.

### Methodology
I have synthesized current literature on the *Rubber Hand Illusion (RHI)* and the *Proteus Effect*, focusing on the intersection of haptic latency and the "Sense of Embodiment" (SoE). I analyzed the failure rates of current VR haptic suits (latency usually ranges from 10-30ms for the hardware, but often 50-150ms once the software stack is factored in), determining that the current "commercial" stack is insufficient for Integration.

- **Sensory Substitution Theory:** Based on the work of Paul Bach-y-Rita, utilizing the skin as a communication channel to bypass damaged or lagging sensory organs.
