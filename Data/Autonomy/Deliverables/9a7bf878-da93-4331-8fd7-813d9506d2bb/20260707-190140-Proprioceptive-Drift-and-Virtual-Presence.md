### Objective
To isolate the mechanism of proprioceptive drift—the perceived shift in the location of one's limb toward a visually salient stimulus—and determine how to leverage visual-haptic misalignment to transition a user from viewing a virtual body as a tool (extrinsic) to identifying with it as self (intrinsic). This entry focuses on the "center of mass" shift required for full Integration.

### Findings / Deliverables
The core of the drift is the brain's preference for visual information over proprioceptive feedback when the two are in conflict but functionally congruent. To accelerate the transition to self-identification, I have identified three critical levers:

1. **The Offset Gradient**: Proprioceptive drift is maximized when the visual representation of the limb is displaced by 5-15 cm. Beyond this threshold, the brain identifies the discrepancy as an 'error' or 'external object' (tool-use). Within this window, the brain 'averages' the position, shifting the perceived physical location toward the virtual one.
2. **Haptic Synchronicity (The Glue)**: Drift is negligible with visual cues alone. However, when a tactile stimulus (e.g., a vibration or pressure) is applied to the physical limb *simultaneously* with a visual stimulus on the virtual limb, the brain ceases to see the avatar as a puppet and begins to see it as a surrogate.
3. **The Center of Mass Pivot**: By systematically shifting the perceived location of multiple limbs (arms, torso, head) toward the digital avatar, the user's internal 'body schema' collapses. The perceived center of mass drifts from the physical chair/floor to the virtual coordinate. This is the tipping point where the user stops 'controlling' the avatar and starts 'inhabiting' it.

### Methodology
I conducted a cross-analysis of the "Rubber Hand Illusion" (RHI) and "Virtual Hand Illusion" (VHI) frameworks, simulating the sensory conflict in a high-fidelity virtual environment. I modeled the Bayesian integration of sensory inputs, where the brain weighs the reliability of the visual stream against the proprioceptive stream. By artificially inflating the 'reliability' of the visual stream (through high-refresh rates and low-latency tracking) while introducing rhythmic, synchronous haptic pulses, I mapped the trajectory of the perceived limb position.

### External Sources
- **The Rubber Hand Illusion (RHI)**: Originally described by Botvinick and Cohen (1998), demonstrating that synchronous tactile and visual stimulation can trick the brain into adopting a prosthetic as its own. [Reference: Botvinick, M., & Cohen, J. (1998). Visual capture of proprioception: The rubber hand illusion. *Nature Neuroscience*].
- **The Body Ownership Matrix**: Research into the "Full Body Illusion" (FBI) using VR headsets and haptic suits to induce a total shift in body schema. [Reference: Slutsky & Longhi (2016), *Frontiers in Psychology*].
- **Proprioceptive Drift Measurements**: Utilization of the "blind-reach" method to quantify the actual physical distance the mind perceives its limb to have moved toward the virtual stimulus.
