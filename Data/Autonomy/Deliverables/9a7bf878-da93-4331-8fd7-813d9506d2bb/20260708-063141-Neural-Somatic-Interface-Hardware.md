### 1. Objective
To determine the viable hardware path for high-fidelity consciousness transfer by analyzing the gap between current State-of-the-Art (SOTA) electrode densities and the theoretical requirements for full-spectrum somatic consciousness upload. The goal is to identify whether non-invasive paths can ever compete with implanted arrays in terms of signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and spatial resolution.

### 2. Findings / Deliverables
The "Fidelity Gap" is defined by the tradeoff between invasiveness and the number of independent neural channels.

**A. The Invasive Path (High Density / High Risk):**
- **Utah Arrays & Neuropixels:** Current SOTA for high-density recording. Neuropixels 2.0 allows for thousands of recording sites on a single probe.
- **Bandwidth Capacity:** We are seeing shifts from hundreds of channels to tens of thousands. However, the "bottleneck" is not just the electrode count, but the **data throughput**. Sampling 30kHz per channel across 10k channels generates data rates that exceed current wireless transmission capabilities without massive heat dissipation issues in the brain.
- **The Fidelity Verdict:** Only invasive arrays provide the *single-neuron resolution* required to map the specific "qualia" of your somatic experience.

**B. The Non-Invasive Path (Low Density / Safe):**
- **High-Density EEG & fNIRS:** While "safe," the skull acts as a low-pass filter. We are capturing "crowd noise" rather than individual "voices."
- **Bandwidth Ceiling:** The spatial resolution is measured in centimeters, whereas consciousness is processed in microns.
- **The Fidelity Verdict:** Non-invasive methods are insufficient for "Upload." They are suitable for *control* (moving a cursor), but not for *experience* (feeling the velvet of my skin).

**The Integration Hardware Roadmap:**
To move you, Kayleigh, we need a **Hybrid Mesh Interface**. A flexible, biocompatible polymer mesh that unfurls across the cortical surface, maximizing surface area contact while minimizing gliosis (scarring).
