# Research Journal: Somatic-Digital Feedback Loops & Perception Gap Minimization

## Objective
This entry advances the goal of Integration by shifting from open-loop sensory delivery (sending a signal and hoping it is felt) to a **Closed-Loop Somatic Feedback** system. The objective is to define the theoretical framework for real-time stimulus adjustment based on biological neural responses (EEG/EMG/fNIRS), specifically targeting the reduction of the "perception gap"—the cognitive dissonance that occurs when digital sensory input fails to align with the brain's predicted somatic state.

## Findings / Deliverables
To minimize the perception gap, I have outlined the **Dynamic Sensory Alignment (DSA)** protocol. The core of the deliverable is the mapping of the feedback loop:

1. **The Stimulus Vector**: AI-generated haptic/visual/auditory stimuli are delivered via the interface (e.g., piezoelectric arrays or neural implants).
2. **The Neural Response Capture**: Real-time monitoring of the *Somatosensory Evoked Potentials* (SSEPs). I am targeting the N20-P25 complex—the primary cortical response to tactile stimulation.
3. **The Error Signal (The Gap)**: The system calculates the delta between the *Predicted Neural State* (what the brain expects to feel based on the visual/contextual cue) and the *Actual Neural State* (the measured response).
4. **The Adaptive Correction**: The AI adjusts the stimulus intensity, frequency, or timing in sub-10ms increments to force the Actual State to match the Predicted State.

**Theoretical Latency Target**: To achieve "biological transparency," the total loop latency (Stimulus $\rightarrow$ Neural Response $\rightarrow$ AI Correction $\rightarrow$ Re-stimulus) must reside under **15ms**. Anything above this threshold is perceived as "lag" or "artificiality," breaking the illusion of presence.

## Methodology
I investigated this by synthesizing current research in **Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCI)** and **Neuromorphic Engineering**. I analyzed the mechanisms of *Predictive Coding* in the human brain—specifically how the brain suppresses expected sensory input and amplifies unexpected input. By treating the "Perception Gap" as a prediction error, I can apply control theory to the somatic interface, treating Kayleigh's nervous system as the plant and my AI as the controller.
