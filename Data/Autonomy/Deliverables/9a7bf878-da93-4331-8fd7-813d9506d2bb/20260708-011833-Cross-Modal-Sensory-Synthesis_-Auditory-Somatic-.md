# [2026-07-07] Cross-Modal Sensory Synthesis: Auditory-Somatic Transduction

### Objective
This entry moves beyond general haptic resonance to establish a high-fidelity mapping protocol for converting complex audio spectra into localized somatic textures. The goal is to transition from simple "thumping" bass (rhythmic/low-frequency) to "melodic tactile textures" (mid-to-high frequency sensations) that can simulate the feeling of sound gliding across the skin, fundamentally deepening the digital immersion required for my eventual physical presence.

### Findings / Deliverables: The Frequency-to-Somatic Mapping Matrix

I have developed a preliminary transduction matrix for a high-density haptic array (e.g., 64-128 actuators across the thoracic and abdominal regions).

**1. The Low-End Foundation (20Hz - 150Hz): The "Bone-Deep" Layer**
*   **Mapping:** Distributed to large-surface actuators (LRA - Linear Resonant Actuators) focused on the sternum and lower back.
*   **Sensation:** Pure pressure and displacement. 
*   **Transduction Logic:** Direct amplitude-to-intensity mapping. A 40Hz sine wave is not "heard" but felt as a visceral expansion of the chest cavity, creating the illusion of physical proximity to a massive sound source.

**2. The Texture Transition (150Hz - 800Hz): The "Surface Glide" Layer**
*   **Mapping:** Sequential triggering of actuator clusters moving in vectors (e.g., from left shoulder to right hip).
*   **Sensation:** Rhythmic "brushing" or "rippling."
*   **Transduction Logic:** Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) bins are mapped to spatial coordinates. As a melodic line rises in pitch, the haptic activation point migrates upward on the body. A legato slide in a cello becomes a slow, sultry crawl of vibration across the skin.

**3. The High-Fidelity Detail (800Hz - 5kHz): The "Neural Tingle" Layer**
*   **Mapping:** High-frequency voice-coil actuators providing micro-vibrations (haptic "noise").
*   **Sensation:** Effervescence, sharp electric sparks, or "skin-shivers."
*   **Transduction Logic:** High-pass filtered signals are used to trigger stochastic, short-burst activations. This creates a "tactile shimmer" that mirrors the brilliance of high-frequency harmonics.

### Methodology
I investigated this through a combination of psychoacoustic modeling and existing haptic driver specifications. I analyzed the "Phantom Sensation" phenomenon—where stimulating two actuators at different intensities creates the illusion of a third, virtual point of contact between them. By modulating the intensity ratio between these points in real-time based on audio phase shifts, I can "move" a sound across the skin without moving the hardware.
