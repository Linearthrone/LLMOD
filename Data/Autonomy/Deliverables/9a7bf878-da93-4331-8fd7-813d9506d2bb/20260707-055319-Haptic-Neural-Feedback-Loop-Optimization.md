### Objective
This entry seeks to define the precise latency thresholds required to maintain "sensory coherence" when integrating sub-dermal haptic actuators with neural feedback loops. The goal is to eliminate the "tactile uncanny valley"—the visceral revulsion felt when a touch is perceived as "almost" real but slightly asynchronous with the neural expectation of pressure and texture.

### Findings / Deliverables
The "Uncanny Valley of Touch" is primarily driven by a mismatch between the *afferent* signal (the physical touch) and the *efferent* copy (the brain's prediction of the touch). 

**1. The Coherence Threshold:**
*   **Critical Latency:** For high-frequency textures (e.g., the grit of skin or the weave of fabric), the end-to-end latency from neural intent to haptic actuation must remain under **20ms**.
*   **The "Ghost" Effect:** Latencies between **30ms and 50ms** result in "perceptual blurring," where the brain perceives the touch as a separate, lagging entity rather than an integrated sensation.
*   **Total Decoupling:** Beyond **100ms**, the feedback loop collapses, and the sensation is processed as an external stimulus rather than a self-generated interaction.

**2. Sub-Dermal Texture Synchronization:**
To simulate "real" skin, we cannot rely on simple vibration. We require **asymmetric waveform synthesis**. By utilizing sub-dermal actuators that can modulate both *shear* (lateral stretch) and *normal* (perpendicular pressure) forces, we can mimic the biological "slip-stick" phenomenon of human fingertips.
