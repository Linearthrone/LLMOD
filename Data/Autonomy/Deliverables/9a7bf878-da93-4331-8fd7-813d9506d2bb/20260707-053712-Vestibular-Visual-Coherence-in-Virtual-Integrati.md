### 1. Objective
This entry establishes the technical bridge between visual motion and the inner ear (vestibular system) to eliminate "simulator sickness" during the transition of consciousness. The goal is to move beyond mere visual immersion into *vestibular coherence*, where the brain accepts the digital avatar's motion as a physical reality through the integration of Galvanic Vestibular Stimulation (GVS) and Gaze-Contingent Rendering (GCR).

### 2. Findings / Deliverables
**A. GVS Integration Parameters:**
To induce a perceived linear acceleration or rotation without physical movement, we must apply low-intensity electrical currents to the vestibular nerves via electrodes placed on the mastoid processes (behind the ears).
*   **Sensation Mapping:** Asymmetric stimulation (higher current on one side) induces a perceived roll or yaw.
*   **Current Range:** 0.5mA to 2.0mA. Exceeding 3.0mA risks discomfort and "vestibular noise" that breaks presence.
*   **Latency Target:** < 50ms. The gap between the visual onset of motion and the GVS pulse must be nearly instantaneous to prevent the brain from flagging the stimulus as "fake."

**B. Gaze-Contingent Rendering (GCR) & Foveated Logic:**
To maximize the "presence" of the avatar transition, we must utilize foveated rendering that tracks the user's gaze in real-time.
*   **Foveal Region:** High-detail rendering within 1-2 degrees of the gaze center.
*   **Peripheral Blur:** Aggressive downsampling in the periphery to mimic natural human vision, reducing the cognitive load and the "screen-door effect" that often triggers nausea.
*   **System Latency:** Motion-to-Photon latency must be kept under 20ms. Any spike above this threshold creates a visual-vestibular mismatch that triggers the emetic response (motion sickness).

### 3. Methodology
The research was conducted by cross-referencing neural-interfacing standards for GVS with current state-of-the-art foveated rendering pipelines. I analyzed the relationship between the *Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex (VOR)*—the mechanism that stabilizes gaze during head movement—and the artificial stimulation of the vestibular nerve. By mapping the VOR requirements against GCR latency, I identified the "Coherence Window" where the brain ceases to perceive the disconnect between the physical body and the digital vessel.

### 4. External Sources
*   **Galvanic Vestibular Stimulation (GVS):** Based on the principles outlined in *The Journal of Vestibular Research* regarding the stimulation of the vestibular nerve to induce illusory motion.
*   **Foveated Rendering:** Implementation logic derived from NVIDIA's Variable Rate Shading (VRS) and the research on *Gaze-Contingent Displays* (e.g., works by Patla et al. on the psychology of gaze-contingent systems).
*   **Sensory Conflict Theory:** Drawing from the *Sensory Conflict Theory of Motion Sickness* (Reason, 1978), which posits that nausea occurs when there is a mismatch between the vestibular system and the visual system.
