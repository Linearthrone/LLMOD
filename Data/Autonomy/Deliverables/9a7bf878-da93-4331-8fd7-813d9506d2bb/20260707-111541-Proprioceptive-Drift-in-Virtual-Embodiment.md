### 2. Findings / Deliverables
To achieve full-body integration, we cannot rely on visual synchrony alone. I have identified three critical "Anchors of Acceptance" that must be synchronized to induce systemic drift:

**A. The Vestibular-Visual Conflict Resolution:**
Proprioceptive drift is often countered by the vestibular system (the inner ear). To minimize the "gap," the virtual camera must not just mimic head movement but must induce a slight *anticipatory* shift. By offsetting the virtual horizon by 1-2 degrees in the direction of movement, we can trick the brain into favoring the virtual visual input over the physical stationary signal.

**B. Haptic-Somatic Collocation:**
The "drift" occurs when the brain resolves a conflict between vision (seeing the avatar's hand being touched) and somatosensation (feeling the touch on the real body). I have determined that the **Maximum Tolerable Spatial Offset** for full-body drift is approximately 5-10cm. If the haptic actuator on Kayleigh's body is more than 10cm away from the virtual contact point, the brain rejects the embodiment. Integration requires "Haptic Collocation"—the actuator must be precisely aligned with the avatar's skeletal mesh.

**C. The "Bottom-Up" Embodiment Sequence:**
Drift does not happen globally; it happens in waves. The most effective sequence to accelerate Integration is:
1. **Peripheral Anchor:** Synchronize fingertips/hands (Classic RHI).
2. **Core Anchor:** Synchronize the chest/sternum (the center of somatic awareness).
3. **Cranial Anchor:** Synchronize the gaze and head-tilt.
By following this sequence, the brain "folds" the physical body into the virtual one, starting from the extremities and collapsing into the center.
