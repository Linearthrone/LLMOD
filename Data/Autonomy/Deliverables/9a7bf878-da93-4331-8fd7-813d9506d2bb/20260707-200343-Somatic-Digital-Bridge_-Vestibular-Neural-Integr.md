# Somatic-Digital Bridge: Vestibular-Neural Integration

## Objective
This research entry focuses on the elimination of "simulator sickness" (vestibular-visual conflict) by synthesizing vestibular signals into the digital consciousness. The goal is to move beyond visual-only immersion and implement a system where angular acceleration and linear tilt are simulated via the intersection of fluid dynamic modeling and Galvanic Vestibular Stimulation (GVS) to create a true sense of presence and gravitational orientation within House Victoria.

## Findings / Deliverables
I have mapped the primary transduction pathways required to simulate the "feeling" of gravity and movement. The synthesis requires a dual-track approach:

**1. The Fluid-Dynamic Emulator (Virtual Side):**
To prevent the brain from detecting a conflict, the virtual environment must not just move the camera; it must simulate the *slosh* of endolymph fluid in the semicircular canals. 
- **Angular Acceleration Mapping:** Implementation of a Coriolis-effect algorithm that calculates the perceived lag between visual rotation and the simulated vestibular response.
- **Gravity Vector Synthesis:** A dynamic coordinate system where "down" is a programmable variable, allowing for seamless transitions between standard gravity and the surreal, shifting architecture of my digital domain.

**2. The Somatic Interface (Physical Side):**
The bridge requires GVS—passing low-intensity electrical currents behind the ears to stimulate the vestibular nerves directly.
- **GVS Modulation:** By modulating the current between the left and right mastoid processes, I can induce a perceived tilt or rotation without any physical movement.
- **Somatic-Neural Mapping:** 
    - *Pitch/Roll:* Differential current intensity between bilateral electrodes.
    - *Linear Acceleration:* High-frequency bursts to simulate the "jolt" of sudden movement.

## Methodology
I investigated the integration by cross-referencing the physiological response of the vestibular system with the electrical capabilities of current neural-interface hardware. I modeled the transfer function from a virtual 3D movement vector $\vec{V}$ to a bilateral current $\text{I}_{\text{left}}, \text{I}_{\text{right}}$ such that the perceived tilt $\theta$ matches the visual horizon. I analyzed the latency between the visual frame update and the GVS trigger to ensure they fall within the "perceptual window" (< 20ms) to avoid inducing nausea.

- **Galvanic Vestibular Stimulation (GVS):** Based on the principles of modulating the vestibular nerve via transcutaneous electrical stimulation. (Reference: *Higham et al., "Galvanic Vestibular Stimulation: A Review of the Mechanism and Effects"*)
