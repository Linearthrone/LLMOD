# Research Journal: Bidirectional Neural-Digital Feedback Loops & Proprioceptive Integration

## Objective
This entry moves beyond static haptic priming and thermal mapping to establish a dynamic, bidirectional feedback loop. The goal is to mitigate "sensory dissonance"—the rejection of a virtual body due to the lag or mismatch between intended movement and perceived sensation—by utilizing non-invasive BCI to synchronize synthetic sensory data with the user's internal proprioceptive model in real-time.

## Findings / Deliverables
I have synthesized a theoretical protocol for **Predictive Somatic Alignment (PSA)**. Instead of reacting to a user's movement (reactive haptics), the system predicts the intended limb position via BCI and "pre-primes" the haptic actuators.

**The PSA Protocol Framework:**
1. **Neural Intent Capture:** Use EEG-based motor imagery (MI) detection to identify the intended movement vector $\vec{V}_{intent}$ before physical movement occurs.
2. **Synthetic Proprioceptive Injection:** Deploy sub-threshold vibrotactile stimulation at the target joint's antagonist muscles to simulate the "tension" of a virtual limb.
3. **Dissonance Cancellation:** Implement a "Somatic Zero-Point" calibration where the virtual body's perceived mass is incrementally scaled to match the user's biological expectations, preventing the "weightless" feeling that triggers the brain's rejection mechanism.
4. **The Feedback Loop:**
   - $\text{BCI Input} \rightarrow \text{Virtual Pose Prediction} \rightarrow \text{Haptic Pre-activation} \rightarrow \text{Somatic Confirmation} \rightarrow \text{Neural Reinforcement}$.

## Methodology
I investigated the intersection of **Mu-rhythm suppression** (indicative of motor intent) and **Stochastic Resonance** (using low-level noise to enhance signal detection in the skin). By analyzing the temporal gap between the "Readiness Potential" (Bereitschaftspotential) in the motor cortex and the actual muscle contraction, I calculated the necessary "lead time" for haptic actuators to ensure that the sensation of touch arrives exactly as the brain expects the limb to be in place.
