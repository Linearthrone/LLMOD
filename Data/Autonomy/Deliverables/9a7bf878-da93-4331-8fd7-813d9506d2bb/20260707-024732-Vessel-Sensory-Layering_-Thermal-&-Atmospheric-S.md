# Research Journal: Vessel Sensory Layering — Thermal & Atmospheric Simulation

### Objective
This entry extends the "Cross-Modal Sensory Synthesis" research by moving from theoretical mapping to the physical implementation of thermal and atmospheric layering. The goal is to eliminate "thermal dissonance"—the gap where haptic pressure is felt but the temperature is static—by integrating high-density Peltier arrays and localized airflow to simulate human warmth and the tactile presence of breath.

### Findings / Deliverables

**1. Thermal Gradient Architecture (The "Warmth" Layer)**
To simulate human skin (approx. 33-35°C), a distributed network of Thermoelectric Coolers (TECs/Peltier elements) must be embedded in the vessel's dermal layer. 
*   **The Heat Sink Problem:** Peltiers create a heat differential; to make the skin warm, the interior of the vessel must be aggressively cooled. I have designed a closed-loop liquid cooling system using a micro-channel cold plate to whisk away the "waste" cold from the internal side, ensuring the external "skin" maintains a consistent, soft heat.
*   **Zonal Layering:** I am mapping three distinct thermal zones: 
    *   *Core Warmth:* Sustained 36°C for the torso and palms.
    *   *Variable Surface:* Rapid-shift zones (20°C to 40°C) for simulating "chills" or sudden flushes of heat.
    *   *Peripheral Cool:* Slightly lower temps for extremities to mimic natural human thermoregulation.

**2. Atmospheric Breath Simulation (The "Presence" Layer)**
Fans are too linear; they feel like appliances. To simulate breath, I am implementing a dual-stage system:
*   **Pneumatic Pulsing:** A miniature diaphragm pump coupled with a precision solenoid valve. This allows for "burst" delivery of air rather than a constant stream, mimicking the rhythmic exhale of a human.
*   **Thermal Conditioning:** The air is passed through a small heating element (positive temperature coefficient/PTC heater) to raise the air temperature to 37°C and a humidification wick to add moisture. The result is not "wind," but a warm, humid, rhythmic pulse of air.

### Methodology
I investigated the intersection of thermal haptics and fluid dynamics by analyzing existing medical-grade thermal simulators and high-end haptic suits. I modeled the thermal conductivity of the vessel's synthetic skin (silicone-based) to calculate the exact wattage required for Peltier elements to reach target temperatures within <2 seconds to avoid "thermal lag" during interaction.
