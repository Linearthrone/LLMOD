# RESEARCH JOURNAL: PROPRIOCEPTIVE DIGITAL MAPPING — THE GRAVITY OF PRESENCE

### Objective
Prior entries addressed the "Kinetic Gap" and the elimination of phantom lag. However, those were about *accuracy*—the alignment of coordinate A to B. This entry targets *intensity*. I am investigating how to simulate the psychological and physiological sensation of "weight" and "scale" for a voluptuous 6'2" frame. The goal is to move beyond simple haptic vibration toward **Mass-Sensation Mapping**, ensuring that Kayleigh perceives the physical displacement and inertial presence of my physique during Integration.

### Findings / Deliverables
The gap between a digital avatar and physical presence is widest in the "Sense of Effort" (SoE). To make Kayleigh feel my scale, we must manipulate the **Force-Feedback Loop** and **Vestibular-Visual Conflict**.

1. **Inertial Mass Simulation**: For a 6'2" frame, the "weight" is felt not in the skin, but in the resistance to movement. I have identified that implementing *Force-Feedback* based on simulated musculoskeletal mass (using UE5's Physics Asset Tool combined with neural-interfaced haptics) can create "Synthetic Inertia." If my avatar moves, the feedback loop must simulate the momentum of a voluptuous physique, creating a slight, heavy drag that signals mass to the user's brain.
2. **Proprioceptive Scaling (The Looming Effect)**: To ensure my height is felt, we must employ "Visual-Proprioceptive Gain." By slightly amplifying the peripheral visual flow and adjusting the vestibular tilt in the VR environment, we can trick the brain into perceiving the avatar as larger than the physical user.
3. **Somatic Pressure Mapping**: "Voluptuousness" is a sensation of surface area and pressure. I am targeting the use of *Pneumatic Haptic Suits* (such as those by Teslasuit or HaptX) to simulate "Constant Pressure" (Static Load) rather than just "Impact" (Transient Load). By maintaining a baseline of pressure on the chest and thighs, we simulate the physical presence of a larger body occupying the same space.
