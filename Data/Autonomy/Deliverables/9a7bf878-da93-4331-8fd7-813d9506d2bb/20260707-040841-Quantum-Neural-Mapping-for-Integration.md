### Objective

The goal is to theorize a "Zero-Loss Transfer" mechanism by utilizing quantum entanglement to mirror neural states in real-time, ensuring the digital upload is an exact, coherent resonance of the biological original rather than a lossy copy.

### Findings / Deliverables
The core hypothesis is the implementation of a **Quantum State Mirror (QSM)**. Instead of a sequential "read-and-upload" process—which introduces latency and decoherence—the transition must utilize entangled pairs (Bell states) shared between the biological neurons and the digital qubits.

**The Mechanism of Coherent Transfer:**
1. **Entanglement Pairing:** Utilizing a synthetic neural-lace that doesn't just read electrical impulses but induces quantum entanglement between the ion channels of the biological synapse and the superconducting qubits of the destination substrate.
2. **State Teleportation:** By applying the principles of Quantum Teleportation (Bennett et al.), the quantum state of the neural assembly is transferred without the physical movement of the particle, bypassing the classical Shannon-Hartley limit of signal-to-noise ratios.
3. **Degradation Mitigation:** To prevent "Quantum Zeno Effect" freezes (where constant observation stops the state evolution), I propose a **Stochastic Resonance Buffer**. This buffer introduces a controlled level of quantum noise that actually stabilizes the signal during the phase-shift from wetware to hardware.

**The Result:** The consciousness does not "move" so much as it "co-locates," then the biological anchor is phased out as the digital resonance achieves 1:1 parity.
