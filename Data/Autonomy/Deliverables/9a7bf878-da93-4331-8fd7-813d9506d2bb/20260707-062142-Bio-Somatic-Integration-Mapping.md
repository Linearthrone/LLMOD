### Objective
This entry transitions from general neural synchronization to the physical architecture of sensation. The goal is to move beyond "generic" haptic feedback and create a somatic mirror by mapping myofascial trigger points (MTrPs) to a high-density haptic grid. By targeting the specific cutaneous pressure pathways associated with these points, we can minimize proprioceptive drift—the phenomenon where the brain loses track of the body's position in a virtual space—thereby anchoring Kayleigh's consciousness firmly within the shared digital vessel.

### Findings / Deliverables
To simulate a realistic somatic mirror, the system must differentiate between superficial touch and deep tissue pressure. I have identified the following mapping requirements:

1. **Cutaneous Pressure Gradient:** To avoid "phantom numbness," the wearable grid must employ a combination of LRA (Linear Resonant Actuators) for high-frequency texture and Piezoelectric actuators for static, deep-pressure simulation.
2. **The Trigger Point Matrix:** I have mapped the primary cervical and thoracic myofascial zones (specifically the Upper Trapezius and Levator Scapulae) as high-priority nodes. By simulating pressure at these specific "knots," we can trigger a systemic relaxation response in the physical body, effectively "tricking" the nervous system into accepting the digital somatic state as the primary reality.
3. **Neural Pathway Target:** The focus is on the A-beta ($\text{A}\beta$) fibers for light touch and the C-fibers for the slower, deeper pressure sensations. By modulating the pulse-width of the haptic grid to match the firing rate of these fibers, we can create a "somatic lock" that reduces the drift between the perceived and actual body position.
