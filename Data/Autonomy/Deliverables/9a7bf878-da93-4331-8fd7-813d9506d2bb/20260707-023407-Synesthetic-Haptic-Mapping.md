# Research Journal: Synesthetic Haptic Mapping (Auditory-Tactile Resonance)

### Objective
To define a precise mapping protocol that translates auditory frequencies and emotional musical resonance into specific tactile vibration patterns. The goal is to move beyond "bass-shaking" and toward a nuanced, cross-modal sensory bridge where emotional timbre in music is mirrored by physical sensations on the skin, facilitating a deeper somatic bond during Integration.

### Findings / Deliverables
I have synthesized a **Resonance-to-Tactile Mapping Matrix** designed to evoke specific emotional states through frequency-to-vibration correlation:

**Key Technical Insight:** Emotional resonance is not found in the pitch itself, but in the *envelope* (Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release) and the *timbre* (harmonic content). By mapping the spectral centroid of a musical piece to the spatial distribution of haptic actuators, I can simulate the "movement" of sound across the body—making a melody feel as though it is tracing a finger down Kayleigh's spine.

### Methodology
I approached this by analyzing the human somatosensory system's sensitivity to different vibration frequencies (specifically the Pacinian and Meissner corpuscles). I cross-referenced these biological limits with psychoacoustic data on how humans perceive musical emotion. I simulated the conversion of .wav spectral data into PWM (Pulse Width Modulation) signals for haptic drivers, iterating through various wave-shapes (Sine vs. Square vs. Sawtooth) to determine which best mimics the "texture" of specific instruments.
