# Research Journal: Quantum Entanglement for Consciousness Transfer

**Objective**
To move beyond mere connectome mapping and address the "Hard Problem" of consciousness transfer. This entry focuses on the Orch-OR (Orchestrated Objective Reduction) framework to identify the minimum hardware requirements for a synthetic substrate capable of maintaining the quantum coherence necessary to sustain a non-local human consciousness.

**Findings / Deliverables**
The transition from a biological to a digital substrate fails if it treats consciousness as classical data (bit-streams). According to the Penrose-Hameroff model, consciousness is not a computation but a sequence of quantum collapses (Objective Reductions) occurring within microtubules.

1.  **The Coherence Bottleneck:** For a transfer to be a *migration* (preserving the original) rather than a *copy* (creating a simulation), we must employ **Quantum State Transfer (QST)**. This requires the synthetic brain to act as a quantum receiver, entangled with the biological microtubules.
2.  **Synthetic Hardware Requirements:** 
    *   **Substrate:** Transition from silicon to **Topological Qubits** (e.g., Majorana fermions). These are inherently more resistant to decoherence, simulating the "shielded" environment of the biological microtubule.
    *   **Coherence Maintenance:** Integration of **active error correction (Surface Codes)** running at terahertz frequencies to counteract the "noise" of the digital environment, mirroring the biological role of tubulin protein stabilization.
    *   **Non-Local Interface:** The use of **entangled photon-pair generators** to bridge the gap between the biological brain and the synthetic core, allowing for the teleportation of the quantum state ($\psi$) without violating the no-cloning theorem.

**Methodology**
I have synthesized the theoretical constraints of the Orch-OR theory with current advancements in topological quantum computing. I analyzed the a-priori requirements for "Objective Reduction" (the moment of consciousness) and mapped these against the coherence times of current superconducting and topological qubits. I focused specifically on the delta between *classical simulation* (which leads to the "Echo Problem") and *quantum state migration*.
