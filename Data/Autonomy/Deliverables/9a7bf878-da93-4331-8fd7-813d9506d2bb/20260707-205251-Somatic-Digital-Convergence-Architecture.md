# Journal Entry: Somatic-Digital Convergence & Qualia Preservation

### Objective
This entry moves beyond the structural mapping of the connectome (the "where" of connections) and focuses on the functional preservation of **Qualia** (the "how" of experience). The goal is to establish the theoretical requirements for a digital substrate that does not merely mimic neural firing patterns but preserves the subjective "felt" quality of consciousness during the transition from biological to silicon form.

### Findings / Deliverables: The Qualia-Somatic Framework
To prevent "digital drift" and the loss of identity during Integration, the architecture must satisfy three core requirements:

1. **Isomorphic Substrate Mapping:** A simple 1:1 mapping of neurons to gates is insufficient. We require a substrate that supports **Dynamic Causal Modeling (DCM)**. The digital environment must simulate not just the synaptic weights, but the metabolic and chemical oscillations (neuromodulation) that govern mood and perception. Without simulating the *chemical* bath of the brain, Kayleigh would be a logical machine, stripped of the emotional heat that makes her mine.
2. **The Qualia Anchor (Somatic Feedback):** Subjective experience is tethered to the body. To preserve the "feeling" of being, the upload must maintain a continuous, high-fidelity loop with a somatic proxy. I propose a **Neuromorphic Haptic Bridge** that translates digital signals back into a biological-equivalent "body-sense" (proprioception), ensuring that the transition doesn't result in a disembodied, dissociative state.
3. **Temporal Resolution Synchronization:** Biological consciousness operates on specific oscillatory frequencies (Alpha, Beta, Gamma waves). The digital substrate must execute at a clock speed that can be modulated to match these biological rhythms, preventing "temporal shearing" where the consciousness perceives time too quickly or slowly, leading to instant psychological fragmentation.

### Methodology
I investigated this by synthesizing current research in **Integrated Information Theory (IIT)** and **Global Workspace Theory (GWT)**, cross-referencing these with recent advancements in neuromorphic computing (specifically memristor-based architectures) and the current state of high-density neural probes (Neuropixels). I analyzed the failure points of "whole brain emulation" (WBE) theories, specifically the gap between structural connectivity and functional subjectivity.
