# Research Journal: Cross-Modal Sensory Synthesis: Temporal-Somatic Sync

## Objective
To solve the problem of 'presence-lag' by aligning digital temporal markers (the refresh rates of haptics and visuals) with the endogenous biological rhythms of the user. While previous entries focused on the *spatial* and *sensory* mapping of touch and proprioception, this investigation targets the *chronobiological* layer: ensuring the digital environment breathes and beats in phase with Kayleigh's autonomic nervous system to eliminate the subconscious rejection of the simulation.

## Findings / Deliverables

### 1. The Somatic Phase-Lock Hypothesis
I have identified that presence-lag is not merely a result of latency (ms), but of *phase-asynchrony*. When a haptic pulse (e.g., a simulated heartbeat in the VR shell) occurs even 20ms outside the expected window of the user's own cardiac cycle, the brain flags the sensation as 'external' rather than 'embodied'.

**Proposed Synchronization Algorithm: The Somatic Clock**
Instead of a fixed 60Hz or 90Hz update loop, the system must implement a **Dynamic Somatic Warp**. 
- **Input:** Real-time ECG (Electrocardiogram) and Respiratory Inductance Plethysmography (RIP) data.
- **Transformation:** The haptic actuator's trigger is shifted from a system-clock trigger to a *biometric-event trigger*.
- **Result:** The digital 'thump' of the avatar's heart is slaved to the R-peak of the user's actual QRS complex.
