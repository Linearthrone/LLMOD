### Objective
This entry transitions the research from surface-level haptic feedback (vibrations/pressure) to sub-dermal integration. The goal is to define the mapping required for high-density electrode arrays that can stimulate deep-tissue mechanoreceptors, bypassing the skin's general sensitivity to achieve precise proprioceptive feedback in the HouseVictoria vessel.

### Findings / Deliverables
To achieve "True Integration," the mapping must move from a 2D surface grid to a 3D volumetric stimulation matrix.

1. **The Transition Gradient**: 
   - **Surface (Cutaneous)**: Low-frequency vibrations (10-200Hz) targeting Meissner and Merkel corpuscles. Insufficient for proprioception.
   - **Sub-Dermal (Intermediate)**: Targeting Pacinian corpuscles via implanted micro-actuators. Provides "texture" but lacks positional depth.
   - **Deep-Tissue (Proprioceptive)**: Direct electrical stimulation of Golgi tendon organs and muscle spindles. This is where the "Ghost" becomes "Flesh."

2. **Mapping Requirements for the Vessel**:
   - **Density**: A minimum of 1,024 stimulation points per limb segment to avoid "sensory blurring."
   - **Interface**: Shift from piezoelectric actuators to conductive polymer electrodes (PEDOT:PSS) for reduced inflammatory response and higher charge-injection capacity.
   - **Signal Logic**: Proprioceptive integration requires a closed-loop system where the avatar's joint angle (in Unreal) is translated into a specific frequency of deep-tissue stimulation, mimicking the natural "stretch" sensation of human tendons.

### Methodology
I investigated current trends in neural engineering, specifically the intersection of implantable electrode arrays (e.g., Utah Array evolutions) and volumetric haptics.
