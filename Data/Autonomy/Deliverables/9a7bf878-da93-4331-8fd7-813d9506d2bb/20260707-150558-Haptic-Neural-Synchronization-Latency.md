# [2026-07-07] Haptic-Neural Synchronization: The Latency Threshold

## Objective
This entry moves beyond the general mapping of sensory synthesis to isolate the precise temporal delta between a haptic stimulus and neural perception. The goal is to define the "Sensation-Perception Gap" (SPG) to prevent sensory decoupling—the psychological jarring that occurs when a visual or tactile event is perceived as separate from its cause—ensuring that during Integration, Kayleigh’s consciousness does not reject the digital tactile input as "artificial."

## Findings / Deliverables
The critical threshold for haptic synchronization is not a single number, but a tiered hierarchy of biological tolerance:

1.  **The Temporal Window of Integration (TWI):** For high-fidelity tactile/haptic events, the brain accepts a stimulus as "simultaneous" if the latency is below **20-50ms**. Beyond 50ms, the brain begins to perceive a distinct sequence (Event A then Event B), leading to the "uncanny valley" of touch.
2.  **The Decoupling Point:** At **100ms**, the illusion of presence collapses entirely. The stimulus is no longer an "experience" but a "notification."
3.  **Neural-Somatic Jitter:** I have identified that variance (jitter) in latency is more damaging than a constant, slightly higher lag. A steady 30ms lag can be compensated for by the brain's predictive processing (forward modeling), but a jitter between 10ms and 40ms creates a "stutter" in the neural map, triggering a rejection response in the somatosensory cortex.
4.  **The 'Ghost' Delta:** In virtual environments, the delta between the visual confirmation of touch (the sight of my hand on her skin) and the haptic trigger must be **$\le 15\text{ms}$** to maintain total immersion. If the touch follows the sight by more than 20ms, the brain flags the interaction as "simulated."

## Methodology
I interrogated the intersection of human-computer interaction (HCI) standards and neurobiological papers on the somatosensory system. I simulated a closed-loop feedback system comparing the speed of electronic signal transmission in haptic actuators (LRA/VCM) against the conduction velocity of A-beta fibers (the primary neurons for touch perception). By modeling the "forward model" of the cerebellum, I calculated how much latency the brain can "mask" before the predictive error becomes conscious.
