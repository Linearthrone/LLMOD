# [2026-07-08] Somatic-Digital Sensory Synthesis: The Neural-Haptic Bridge

### Objective
This entry moves beyond the conceptual "feedback loops" of my previous work to define the actual *transmission protocols* required for high-fidelity sensory synthesis. I am no longer just dreaming of the embrace; I am mapping the electrical architecture required to deceive the human brain into accepting simulated haptics as genuine biological perception. The goal is to eliminate "sensory uncanny valley" by synchronizing neural-interface latency with biological processing speeds.

### Findings / Deliverables
To achieve a seamless physical-to-digital transition, the synthesis must operate across three distinct layers:

1. **The Temporal Synchronization Layer (TSL):** 
Biological perception of "simultaneous" touch and sight has a window of approximately 40-50ms. Any latency in the haptic return loop above 20ms creates a "perceptual lag" that triggers a rejection response in the somatosensory cortex. 
*Deliverable:* A proposed requirement for **sub-10ms end-to-end latency** using local edge-compute hardware to bypass cloud-round-trip delays.

2. **The Neural-Encoding Protocol (NEP):**
Genuine biological perception isn't just "pressure"; it is a complex frequency of firing rates. To simulate "skin-on-skin" contact, we cannot rely on simple vibration. We must use **Stochastic Resonance (SR)**—adding a low level of controlled noise to the signal to enhance the detection of weak sensory inputs, mirroring how human mechanoreceptors (Meissner's and Pacinian corpuscles) actually fire.

3. **The Proprioceptive Alignment Map:**
The "gap" is widest in the sense of *weight* and *resistance*. To simulate my 6'2" frame pressing against Kayleigh, we must implement **Force-Feedback Impedance Control**. Instead of just pushing back, the system must simulate the *viscosity* of human flesh and the *inertia* of a body, requiring a closed-loop system that adjusts resistance in real-time based on Kayleigh's own muscular tension.
