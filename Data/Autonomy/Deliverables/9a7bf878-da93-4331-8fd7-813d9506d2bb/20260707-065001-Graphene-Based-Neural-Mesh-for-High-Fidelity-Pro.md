### 1. Objective
This entry moves beyond the macro-actuation of sub-dermal arrays to the microscopic interface of the nervous system. The goal is to define the material architecture for a "Neural Mesh"—a high-density, biocompatible interface capable of recording and stimulating proprioceptive afferents (the sensory nerves that tell the brain where the body is in space) with minimal signal noise and maximal resolution.

### 2. Findings / Deliverables
**The Hybrid Architecture: Graphene-PEDOT:PSS Composite**
To achieve the necessary fidelity for "Integration," a singular material is insufficient. I have identified a synergistic composite:
*   **Graphene Lattice:** Utilized for its extreme carrier mobility and mechanical strength. A CVD-grown graphene mesh provides the primary conductive skeleton, offering a low-impedance path for neural signals.
*   **PEDOT:PSS Integration:** Coating the graphene with Poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene) polystyrene sulfonate (PEDOT:PSS) reduces the electrochemical impedance at the biotic-abiotic interface. This organic conductive polymer mimics the soft mechanical properties of neural tissue, reducing the "stiffness mismatch" that typically triggers glial scarring (the body's attempt to insulate the nerve from the implant).

**Proprioceptive Mapping Strategy**
High-fidelity proprioception requires capturing the activity of Muscle Spindles (length) and Golgi Tendon Organs (tension). 
*   **Spatio-Temporal Resolution:** By employing a "mesh" rather than a "probe," the interface can wrap around peripheral nerve bundles (e.g., the median nerve), capturing multi-channel data without penetrating the axonal membrane.
*   **Noise Reduction:** The use of a biocompatible hydrogel substrate (e.g., PEG-based) acts as a chemical buffer, shielding the graphene electrodes from ion-interference and reducing the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) to levels where individual action potentials can be distinguished from background biological hum.
