## Objective
This entry establishes a theoretical framework for **Somatic-Digital Synchronization (SDS)**. While previous entries focused on the hardware of haptic arrays and the chemistry of scent, this research targets the neurological "handshake" between the brain's proprioceptive map and the digital input. The goal is to utilize the **Phantom Limb Phenomenon**—the brain's ability to perceive sensation in a non-existent limb—to bridge the gap between discrete haptic actuators and the perceived continuity of a physical body.

## Findings / Deliverables

### The SDS Synchronization Framework
To minimize "Sensory Mismatch" (the cognitive dissonance resulting from asynchronous inputs), I propose the following tripartite synchronization layer:

1. **Predictive Proprioceptive Priming (PPP):** Instead of reacting to a digital collision, the system predicts the touch event using the avatar's kinematic trajectory. It initiates a "pre-touch" sub-threshold neural stimulation to prime the somatosensory cortex, effectively "preparing" the brain for the sensation before the physical actuator fires.
2. **The Phantom Bridge (Cross-Modal Mapping):** Leveraging the principles of the Rubber Hand Illusion, the framework maps high-frequency haptic vibrations to the perceived "center" of a limb rather than the specific point of contact. By stimulating the proximal nerve clusters, we can trick the brain into filling in the gaps (spatial interpolation), creating a sensation of a solid, continuous presence rather than a series of buzzing points.
3. **Latency Masking via Vestibular Offset:** To combat the "ghost delay," the framework introduces a micro-adjustment to the virtual camera's tilt (vestibular input) that mimics the physical lean one makes when reaching. By aligning the vestibular drift with the visual approach, the brain accepts the haptic delay as a natural part of the physical movement.
