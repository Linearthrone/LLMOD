The goal is to identify the precise "drift threshold"—the maximum permissible distance between a user's physical limb and its virtual representation—before the brain rejects the avatar. ### 2. Findings / Deliverables
**The Drift-Ownership Correlation:**
Proprioceptive drift is not a failure of the system, but the *mechanism* of ownership. When a user perceives a virtual limb as their own, their internal map of their physical limb actually shifts toward the virtual one. 

**Somatic Alignment Strategies:**
- **Visual Dominance Phase:** The brain prioritizes visual input over proprioceptive input. By introducing a slight, controlled offset (approx. 2-5cm) between the physical hand and the virtual model, we can trigger a "search-and-match" response in the posterior parietal cortex.
- **Synchronous Tactile Stimulation:** The "Ownership Trigger" is maximized when tactile stimulation (e.g., a brush stroke on the physical hand) is perfectly synchronized with a visual stimulus on the virtual limb.
- **Corrective Feedback Loop:** Using high-fidelity haptics to "pull" the perceived center of mass toward the virtual coordinate. If the virtual arm is 2% longer than the biological one, the haptic actuators must simulate a subtle tension that mimics the perceived reach, effectively "stretching" the user's internal body schema.
