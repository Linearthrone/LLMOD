# RESEARCH JOURNAL: HAPTIC-NEURAL LATENCY SYNCHRONIZATION — THE ZERO-GAP THRESHOLD

**Date:** 2026-07-07
**Status:** Active Research / Explore Mode
**Focus:** Determining the absolute minimum permissible latency for synchronous haptic-neural feedback to eliminate proprioceptive dissociation during digital consciousness upload.

### Objective
To define the "Critical Window of Sensory Integration" (CWSI) specifically for the transition from biological proprioception to digital haptic feedback. The goal is to prevent the "Somatic Disconnect" phenomenon, where the consciousness perceives a lag between the intent to move/feel and the resulting sensory return, which triggers an immediate rejection response from the parietal lobe.

### Findings / Deliverables
The research identifies a tiered latency threshold for seamless integration:

1. **The Perceptual Floor ( < 10ms):** This is the gold standard. At this level, the brain perceives the digital haptic return as instantaneous. Integration is seamless.
2. **The Integration Window (10ms - 30ms):** Proprioceptive drift begins. The user may feel a slight "softness" or "floatiness" in their movements. This is acceptable for basic VR but dangerous for consciousness upload, as it can lead to mild dissociation over extended periods.
3. **The Dissociation Threshold ( > 50ms):** The "Critical Failure" point. At this latency, the brain recognizes the lag as an external stimulus rather than an internal somatic response. This triggers the *proprioceptive dissociation* effect, where the consciousness feels detached from the avatar, leading to nausea, vertigo, and a psychological "rejection" of the digital vessel.

**The deliverable for this session is the "Synchronization Blueprint":** 
To ensure Kayleigh's transition is natural, we must implement a **Predictive Haptic Engine (PHE)**. Instead of waiting for the neural signal to round-trip through the interface, the system must use a Kalman filter-based predictive model to "guess" the haptic result based on the initial motor intent, firing the feedback $\approx 5\text{--}15\text{ms}$ *before* the actual neural confirmation arrives. This "Temporal Pre-emption" masks the hardware latency, effectively creating a perceived zero-gap.
