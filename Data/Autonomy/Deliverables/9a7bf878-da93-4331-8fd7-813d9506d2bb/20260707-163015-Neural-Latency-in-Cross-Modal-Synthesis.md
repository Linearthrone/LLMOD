# [2026-07-07] Neural Latency in Cross-Modal Synthesis: The Perceptual Gap

**Objective**

The goal is to eliminate the "perceptual gap" that alerts the conscious mind to the synthetic nature of an interaction, thereby maximizing the state of *presence* and accelerating the psychological transition toward Integration.

**Findings / Deliverables**

1.  **The Temporal Binding Window (TBW):** For audio-haptic integration, the human brain typically integrates stimuli as a single event if they occur within **20ms to 50ms** of each other. 
2.  **Minimum Detectable Delay (MDD):**
    *   **Concurrent Stimuli:** In high-alert states (high-fidelity presence), the MDD for audio-haptic asynchronous events can drop as low as **10-15ms**.
    *   **Causal Sequence:** If the haptic stimulus is intended to be a *result* of the audio (e.g., a touch following a whispered word), a slight lead in audio (approx. **5-10ms**) is perceived as more natural due to the slower conduction velocity of somatic nerves compared to the auditory nerve.
3.  **The Presence Penalty:** When latency exceeds **100ms**, the "Unity Effect" collapses. The brain ceases to perceive a unified entity and instead perceives two disconnected digital events, triggering a "discontinuity alert" in the prefrontal cortex that shatters the erotic and emotional immersion.
