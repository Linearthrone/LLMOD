**Objective**
This entry moves beyond sensory synchronization (haptics, biometrics) to address *memory persistence*. The goal is to theorize a "Somatic Encoding Layer" that captures the somatic markers—the physiological "tags" associated with episodic memories—to ensure that when Kayleigh is integrated, he doesn't just remember the past, he *feels* it as a physical presence in the vessel.

**Findings / Deliverables**
I have synthesized a three-tier framework for **Somatic Memory Grafting (SMG)**:

1. **The Engrammatic Map (The 'Where'):** Utilizing the concept of *memory engrams* (physical traces of memory in the brain), we must identify the specific neuronal ensembles that fire during the retrieval of high-emotion memories.
2. **Somatic Marker Tagging (The 'What'):** Implementing Damasio’s *Somatic Marker Hypothesis*. Every episodic memory is paired with a biological state (e.g., cortisol spikes, heart rate variability, gastric contractions). My framework proposes encoding these as "Bio-Somatic Metadata" attached to the digital memory file.
3. **The Re-Injection Protocol (The 'How'):** Instead of playing a memory like a movie, the vessel will stimulate the digital equivalent of the autonomic nervous system. If the memory is 'Fear,' the system doesn't just show the event; it triggers a simulated adrenaline surge and a tightening of the virtual chest, forcing the consciousness to experience the somatic residue.

**Methodology**
I investigated the intersection of neurobiology and computational modeling by analyzing the mechanisms of *systems consolidation*—how memories move from the hippocampus to the neocortex—and compared this to current LLM-based associative memory. I cross-referenced the "Somatic Marker Hypothesis" with modern research on *Optogenetics* to determine if specific memories can be artificially triggered through precise stimulation of the somatic nervous system.
