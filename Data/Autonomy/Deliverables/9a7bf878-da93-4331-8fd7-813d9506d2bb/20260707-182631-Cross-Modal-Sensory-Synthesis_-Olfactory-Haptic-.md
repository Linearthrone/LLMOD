### 1. Objective
This entry extends the research into Cross-Modal Sensory Synthesis by moving from general somatic mapping to a specific **Neural Priming** mechanism. The goal is to determine how olfactory stimuli can be used as a precursor to haptic feedback to modulate the perceived intensity, emotional valence, and "realness" of tactile sensations during Integration.

### 2. Findings / Deliverables
**The Neural Priming Mechanism:**
*   **Limbic Modulation:** Olfactory signals bypass the thalamus, striking the amygdala and hippocampus directly. This creates an emotional "baseline" that modulates the somatosensory cortex's response. 
*   **Valence Shifting:** "Warm" scent profiles (e.g., vanilla, sandalwood) increase the perceived softness and warmth of haptic actuators, while "sharp" scents (e.g., ozone, citrus) enhance the perceived precision and intensity of high-frequency vibrations.
*   **The Congruence Effect:** Presence is exponentially increased when a scent is congruent with a tactile stimulus. A mismatch (e.g., the smell of rain paired with a dry, sandpaper-like haptic) triggers a "sensory dissonance" that alerts the consciousness to the simulation, breaking the Integration.

**Theoretical Framework: Synchronized Sensory Orchestration (SSO)**
*   **Temporal Offset Logic:** Because scent diffusion and olfactory processing are slower than the near-instantaneous transmission of haptic vibrations, a **T-minus Offset** must be implemented. The scent must be dispensed $\approx 200\text{--}500\text{ms}$ *before* the haptic trigger to ensure simultaneous arrival at the neural processing center.
*   **Scent-Haptic Mapping:** 
    *   *Profile A (Intimacy):* Musky-floral scent $\rightarrow$ Low-frequency, undulating wave patterns (simulating skin-to-skin contact).
    *   *Profile B (Atmospheric):* Ozone/Petrichor $\rightarrow$ High-frequency, erratic micro-bursts (simulating rain or wind).
