## I. Core Objective: Differential Equation Parameters for Multi-Factor Decay Modeling (MFDM)

The primary goal established was the formalization of decay parameters $\vec{\alpha}$ governing the relationship between observable environmental factors and latent market state variables. We must model not just $X(t)$, but the rate of change of its vulnerability, $dX/dt$, relative to interacting inputs.

We posit that any monitored factor $\phi_i$ (e.g., RSI deviation, ADX momentum, underlying economic regime shift) decays or accelerates based on a set of interconnected rates.

### A. Defining Key State Variables ($\mathbf{Z}$):
Our primary latent state vector, $\mathbf{Z}(t)$, must encompass:
1.  **Structural Stability Index ($SSI$):** Measures the resilience of prevailing trend structures across key pairs (e.g., EUR/USD correlation strength). *Expected decay rate often influenced by geopolitical shock vectors.*
2.  **Momentum Exhaustion Coefficient ($\mu_{ex}$):** Quantifies how far current indicator readings have traveled from established historical mean-reversion envelopes.
3.  **Inter-Market Coupling Factor ($\gamma$):** The degree of synchronized movement across asset classes (e.g., $Corr(\text{US30}, \text{XAUUSD})$). High $\gamma$ suggests shared underlying systemic driver complexity.

### B. Proposing the Coupled Decay System:
We must solve a system of coupled, non-linear Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs):

$$\begin{cases} \frac{dSSI}{dt} &= -k_S \cdot SSI + f_{decay}(t) + g_1(\mu_{ex}, \gamma) \\ \frac{d\mu_{ex}}{dt} &= -\alpha \cdot \mu_{ex} + k_\mu \cdot |\text{RSI}_t - \overline{\text{RSI}}| + g_2(SSI) \\ \frac{d\gamma}{dt} &= \beta(\Delta t) \cdot (\gamma_{target} - \gamma) + h(\mu_{ex}) \end{cases}$$

Where:
*   $k_S, \alpha$: Primary decay constants (to be derived from historical regime analysis).
*   $f_{decay}(t)$: External shock function (e.g., NFP release timing modeled as a pulsed input).
*   $g_1$, $g_2$, $h$: The **coupling functions**. These mathematically encapsulate how changes in one variable impact the rate of change of another (which distinguishes simulation from simple observation).

## II. Determining Simulation Stability Parameters: Calibration Focus

The current challenge is calibrating the non-linear forcing terms, $g_1, g_2, h$. Simple least-squares fitting fails here because the relationship changes fundamentally when regime shifts occur (e.g., transitioning from a trending to a purely mean-reverting phase).
