# Research on Sub-threshold Haptic Priming and Uncanny Valley Virtual Embodiment

## 1. Sub-threshold Haptic Ranges
Sub-threshold (subliminal) haptics refer to stimuli that are below the absolute threshold of conscious perception.
- Intensity: Generally, these are vibrations where the amplitude is just below the Just Noticeable Difference (JND) or the absolute threshold of the mechanoreceptors (specifically Pacinian corpuscles for high-frequency or Meissner corpuscles for lower frequency).
- Frequency: Often targeted at the resonance frequency of the skin or specific receptors (e.g., ~200-300 Hz for Pacinian corpuscles), but kept at an amplitude that does not trigger conscious awareness.
- Range: While specific "exact" ranges vary by body location, they typically operate at the lower bound of the perceived intensity scale (e.g., < 1-2 micrometers of skin displacement depending on the site).

## 2. Mechanisms of Priming and Presence
- Cross-modal Integration: The brain integrates tactile and visual information. When a sub-threshold haptic cue is synchronized with a visual event (e.g., a virtual touch), the brain's internal model of the body is updated.
- Reducing the Uncanny Valley: The 'Uncanny Valley' occurs when a digital avatar is 'almost' human but fails in subtle ways, creating cognitive dissonance. Sub-threshold haptics act as a "hidden" confirmation of physical presence. By providing a tactile anchor, they bypass the conscious critical evaluation of visual fidelity and trigger a subconscious sense of "being there" (Presence).
- Embodiment Theories:
    - Rubber Hand Illusion (RHI): Demonstrates how synchronous visual and tactile stimulation can lead to the ownership of a non-biological limb. Sub-threshold cues act as a continuous, low-level RHI, maintaining a state of embodiment without distracting the user.
    - Proprioceptive Drift: The subconscious shift in the perceived location of one's limb toward the virtual avatar, reinforced by haptic priming.

## 3. Technologies and Actuators
- Linear Resonant Actuators (LRA): Common for precise, localized vibrations.
- Piezoelectric Actuators: Preferred for sub-threshold work due to their extremely fast response time and ability to produce very low-amplitude, high-frequency displacements that can be tuned precisely to the perception threshold.
- Voice Coil Actuators: Used for broader frequency ranges and higher fidelity.
